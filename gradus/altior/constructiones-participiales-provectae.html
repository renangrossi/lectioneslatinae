Gradus VI · Altior

Constructiones Participiales Provectae

Vidi eum currentem — participium supplementarium, participia catenata, usus substantivus.

PropositaExplicatioRegulaeExemplaErrores CommunesExercitiumSummariumTe Ipsum Proba

Prooemium

Praeter ablātīvum absolūtum et participium concordāns simplex, Latīna habet aliōs ūsūs participiī frequentēs in prōsā prōvectiōre — hodiē eōs cōnsolidāmus.

Post hanc lectionem poteris…

  • Uti participio supplementari post verba perceptionis (video, audio + accusativum + participium)
  • Distinguere participium supplementarium ab AcI (constructio similis, functio distincta)
  • Uti participiis substantive, sine nomine explicito
  • Legere catenas participiales multiplices in prosa densa

Explicatio

Explicatio

Participium, sīcut adiectīvum flexibile, servit multīs officiīs syntacticīs praeter illa iam nōta.

Regulae Grammaticae

Regulae

a) Participium Supplementarium (post Verba Perceptionis)

Post videō, audiō, sentiō, obiectum accūsātīvum + participium (concordāns cum illō obiectō) dēscrībit āctiōnem PERCEPTAM DIRECTĒ: Vīdī puerum currentem. ('I saw the boy RUNNING' — vīdī ipsum āctum currendī, nōn tantum factum abstractum.) Compara cum AcI: Audīvī puerum cecidisse. ('I heard THAT the boy had fallen' — factum reportātum, nōn necessāriō vīsum/audītum dīrēctē.)

b) Participia Catenata

Prōsa dēnsa (Tacitus praesertim) catenat multa participia sequentia: Mīlitēs, hostibus victīs, urbe captā, praedā dīvīsā, domum revertērunt. ('The soldiers, the enemy having been defeated, the city having been captured, the plunder having been divided, returned home' — trēs ablātīvī absolūtī sequentēs.)

c) Usus Substantivus

Participium potest fungī ut nōmen ipsum, sine nōmine explicitō, praesertim in plūrālī: sapientēs ('the wise [men/people]' — ā sapiēns), dicta ('sayings, things said' — ā dictus, neutrum plūrāle), victī ('the defeated [ones]').

d) Distinctio Finalis: Quattuor Usus Simul

ConstructioExemplum
Concordans simplexMīlitēs victī fūgērunt.
Ablativus absolutusHostibus victīs, mīlitēs fūgērunt.
SupplementariumVīdī mīlitēs fugientēs.
SubstantivumVictī fūgērunt (nōn 'mīlitēs victī').

Exempla

Exempla in Usu

  • Vidimus naves appropinquantes. — We saw the ships approaching.
  • Audivi te cantantem. — I heard you singing.
  • Fortes fortuna adiuvat. — Fortune favors the brave. (usus substantivus, proverbium)
  • Milites, hostibus victis, urbe capta, laeti domum redierunt. — The soldiers, the enemy defeated, the city captured, returned home joyfully.
  • Nemo sapientium hoc credidisset. — None of the wise men would have believed this.

Errores Communes

Cave

VitaParticipium supplementarium (video + acc. + part.) confunditur cum AcI, ambo tractati identice.

RectiusParticipium supplementarium indicat perceptionem DIRECTAM actus ('saw him running'); AcI indicat factum REPORTATUM ('heard that he had fallen').

Distinctio semantica subtilis sed reālis: experientia sensualis directa vs. informatio reportata.

VitaParticipium substantivum ('sapientes') tractatur ut requirens semper nomen explicitum concomitans.

RectiusParticipium potest stare solum, functionans ipse ut nomen ('the wise', 'the defeated'), sine nomine explicito necessario.

Haec est extensio naturalis functionis adiectivalis participii — sicut quodvis adiectivum potest fungi substantive in Latina.

Summarium

Recognitio

  • Participium supplementarium (video/audio + acc. + part.): perceptio directa actus.
  • Participia catenata: multiplices ablativi absoluti sequentes in prosa densa.
  • Usus substantivus: participium fungit ut nomen ipsum (sapientes, victi, dicta).
  • Quattuor usus participii nunc omnes noti: concordans, ablativus absolutus, supplementarium, substantivum.
